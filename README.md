# Skydysh

A game for Android: races through an open town, a southern seaside town on a summer evening
seen as town games were drawn early in the century,
its streets grown from a seed without end as they are come to, cars going about them,
people out on the pavements with their dogs, cats by the walls (who mind what she does: a mallet
thrown near them and they are off); squares with fountains or
gardens, trees, benches and pots of flowers along the streets. For now on foot: a brum, a giant of
brick and stone in a maid's dress runs, turns, jumps and slides through it; a car, a boat and
a plane are to come.

The town is lived in by humans and brums. A brum is a shell of brick and stone made to stand
and walk; she is one, and so are the ones she hunts.

It opens, at every start, with a flight in from the sea over the beach and the square to home,
where she stands in the shore road with a golden mallet and brings it down into the glass of the
screen, and the name is typed in the letters of the dispatcher's tube; a touch skips it. The game
begins with the first job, the runaways, straight from the dispatcher's room, which is her head:
walls of her own brick round the street seen through her eyes, what is to be done typed over it;
a touch, her mallet knocks a key, the walls draw back and the street goes on. That one done, she
goes home, and the town is hers: the jobs are at home, on the sofa, and none is urged on her.

A mission lost (the time run out, the folder taken by another): back into her head at once for
the report, and the same task again from where it began, its targets laid out the same, never
from where it was lost. A mission won: no screen; at the top, done and home, and a little green
house over home's, or at the edge of the frame the way it is. The town is hers on the way.
At home, on the sofa, the game is kept and the report read (its time against the mission's best,
kept on the phone); then the screen offers every kind of job, the newest at the top, none pushed
on her: no next in turn, no round of them. A sign of the way back floats over the screen:
touched, it lets the screen go and she gets up, the town hers as long as she likes. A job is
called by its kind, not by a number.

Meters to where the way goes are shown only where they are the game itself: in the hunt, to the
nearest runaway, and on the roofs, to the folder and for each seeker. Elsewhere a word at the top
and a mark show the way: a flat little picture of the place, half see-through, not glass (glass
is for what is touched) — a house for home, a pickup, the factory, the warehouse, the pass, the
village, a paw for the puppy, the vets'. Green, the place is open to her; red, shut for now.

One finger leads her: held down, she runs; moved sideways, she turns; flicked up, she jumps,
flicked down, she slides; a tap is the mallet's. Other fingers, while it leads, press what they
come down on (the pause, the way out of the pickup, a lift's button, a sign), and on bare glass
swing the mallet. Standing, a swipe aside turns her where she stands, no more: held on after it
a moment, she sets off on a curve the way it went; held still, she is off at once. A flick up or
down counts only when it goes more up or down than aside.

Two thumbs are set aside for now (the way they went is kept for later, not offered). The left half of the glass is a stick: where the thumb comes down is its
middle, and pushed a little she walks, further she jogs, all the way she runs, the way it is
pushed being the way she goes as the eye sees it (up the glass into the frame, down it toward
the eye); let go, she stops in a step or two. The right half turns the eye round her; left alone
a while, it comes round behind her again as she goes. Low on the right are her buttons: the
jump (pressed again in the air, she goes on up, over a car or onto what stands a storey up;
gliding, it beats her arms and lifts her, four times a flight), the slide over it, and the
mallet's beside it; a flick up or down on the right half jumps or slides her too. Falling from
high enough, she glides down by herself; run at a ladder, she takes hold and climbs it hand over
hand, and pulled down, the stick lets her go. The mallet, by its button or a tap on the right
half: a brum within reach she strikes, one ahead and not too far she throws at; with none, she
throws it ahead and it comes back. Half the blocks
have a yard open to the street by an arch; the others are shut in, with a fire escape up the back
of a house; the fire escapes are all in the yards. Some houses have a way in, a hall through to
the yard; a few have a lift. The
door of a house with a lift opens by itself when she comes to it or stands at it: inside is
its hall, made from the house's seed, and in its side wall the lift, which is called, opens,
takes her up and lets her out on the roof by itself. On the roof, by the hut's door, a button
shows; pressed, the lift takes her down and the cabin opens onto the hall again. Signs of glass
hang over the ways in and up near her: the lifts' doors, the fire escapes, the ladders from roof
to roof; touched, a sign sends her there by herself, up the ladder, or in and up (or down and
out) by the lift. A button high on the left pauses the game; under the pause, the language (the
phone's own, Russian or English, kept as chosen) and the results: the best time of every job
played, on the screen in her head. The eye behind her keeps out of
the leaves: when a tree's crown or a palm's comes between, it drops under it, or comes in nearer.

Up on the right, the navigator: a small map turned the way the eye looks, the streets drawn
from the town itself (the sea, the beach, the blocks, the fields and the road out), she a blue
dot with a white point her way, and to the mission's mark a line along the streets by the
shortest way through the crossings, and on along the road out. Off it, a new way is found from
where she is, and the line flashes white. In the helicopter, the line goes straight. The faster
she goes, the farther the map shows. Under it, the next turn and how far it is; turn round, if
the way goes off behind her; the place itself, if there is no turn before it. A touch folds it
to a small square, and opens it again; it stays as it was left.

At the wheel, one thumb on a steering wheel, drawn under it where it comes down (anywhere but a
button) and turned as the pickup's wheel is, a knob where the thumb is. Aside, the wheel turns,
gently near the middle, full over at the edge, the less the faster the pickup goes. Up, the
throttle, the higher the faster, from a crawl to the top (in the town no faster than its streets
take, out on the road all it has); the wheel green. Down, the brake, the lower the harder, the
wheel coral; held so standing, after a breath it backs, as fast as it is held low. Up and down
have a wider middle than aside, so turning the wheel does not touch the throttle. In the middle
or let go, it rolls on and slows, and the wheel comes back straight. Otherwise coloured for the
mission (coral for a hunt, turquoise for the folder, amber for a delivery, cream when free). It
turns only as it goes: a turn in the street is made in three. The wheel straight, a pickup a
little off the street's way finds it; the rest is the driver's. The way out up on the right. How fast, up on the left under the pause, out from under the thumb.

She gets in where she comes up to a pickup, any side or end, the nose as well as the doors:
walked against it, at once; stood by it, in a moment; its sign touched, she goes to the nearest of
it.

The town ends at the sea: along its southmost street a promenade with palms and a parapet, steps
down to a beach with sunshades, people lying on the loungers and strolling the sand, and the
water. Into the sea she wades, slowly, and where it is
deep enough she swims high in the water, upright, her head and shoulders out, a slow breast
stroke at the water's top (still, she settles slowly a little lower, never under), as far out
as the open sea lets her; back in the shallows
she stands up and wades out. Home is there, where the game begins: one large house with a tower and a lamp on
top, the same in every town; its sign over the door takes her in, to a room of her own: a sofa
against the back wall, lamps either side, the sea in a frame over it, books, plants. Come up to
the sofa and she sits; the game is kept, and the screen in her head asks which mission now (or
the next one, or just a rest). A touch, and she gets up.

Duty pickups stand at the curbs here and there (not in a hunt), and once the delivery is done,
one at the curb a few steps from home's door. Touch the wheel over one, or stand by it a
moment, on any side, and she gets in. Driving is the easy way about the town, and the fast one, up
to some 130 kilometers an hour on a straight (how fast is shown low on the left). The pickup is
the dispatch's own and does not break: a car in its way, going or parked at a curb, is rammed off, sliding and spinning to a
wreck; a street lamp in its way is felled across the road (the town puts it up again once she
is far off). The button low on the right lets her out. She stands on
whatever she comes down on, the roofs too. When a mission is done the game keeps where it is.

For now, to try: a hunt. Five brums on fire that should not be are loose in the town, each
waiting somewhere far off on a pavement of its own; come near one and it runs. Run it down
close enough and she stops and throws her mallet by itself (a tap throws sooner, or, within
reach, strikes); it knocks the plate off its brow, and the brum goes out and falls apart.
At the top, how far off the nearest still loose is, in meters; in the corners, the five plates
as they are won, and the time left; at the edge of the frame, a burning mark
the way each one out of sight is. A hunt has five minutes: when the last is caught in time, five
more are let loose; when the time runs out first, five again.

The second mission, the folder: dropped from a helicopter somewhere on the roofs, a reward for
it, and three seekers after it too: brums of violet brick with true plates, not on fire, who go
up as she must: into the yard behind its row (under an arch, or through a house's hall), up a
fire escape, and along the roofs. The mallet stops a seeker as it stops a
runaway: broken, it is out of the race.
Up by a lift or a fire escape in a house of the row, then roof to roof: up the iron ladders
where the next roof stands higher, down onto the lower ones, leaping, gliding, beating the air
to rise; taken, she goes down to the street with it by herself. If a brum gets there first,
the mission is played again.

A paid job is paid once, the first time it is done; done again, only its time counts. Over each
briefing a sign of a question floats by the screen: touched, a still page of what lies behind the
mission and what it asks; the same from under the pause while it is played.

The third mission, the delivery: a load waits at the factory with the chimney, west along the
shore road, and is wanted at the store, east along it. A duty pickup to the factory's gate,
stood in the bay painted before it while the crates go on; to the store's, while they come off;
then home.

The fourth, the call out: the road to the village over the hills is blocked in the pass, rocks
and fallen trunks across it. The town ends inland, ten streets up from the shore: past its last
street a low wall, fields with trees about them, and one road out, north up the street by home's
west side, winding as it goes; a ridge of green hills across the whole country, the pass cut
through it for the road; beyond, a village of little houses under red tiles round a square with a
well. A duty pickup up the road (it keeps along the road's bends by itself), through the pass
(what lies across it is shoved off, or knocked aside on foot with the mallet), to the square.

The fifth, the puppy at sea: a call from the beach east of home, past the store, where a crowd
stands at the water's edge looking out at a puppy too tired to swim back. No pay, and no clock on
it but the time it takes. Along the shore road in a duty pickup, down to the beach, out
into the water, right up to it, and she takes it; back to the pickup and to the vets', a white
house with a turquoise band and a paw over its door, three streets up and a block east of home,
the bay painted on the street before it.

The sixth, the tow: out of town at the foot of the ridge, west of the road to the pass, an old
quarry cut into the hill, gravel at its bottom and steps of rock round it; a brum's pickup has
gone down into it and sits sunk in the mud, the brum (slate grey) at its wheel. A duty pickup up
the road out and to it; stopped by it a moment, nose or tail to it, and a rope goes on between
them; then back, the brum's pickup following on the rope, to the brums' workshop at the town's
last street by the road out, a dark brick house with two gates and a turquoise plate over them,
and into the bay painted on the road before it. No pay, no clock but the time it takes.

The seventh, the lights in the hall, paid: the concert hall north of the old town, a portico of
columns on broad steps, three tall doors. In by any of them, up the steps anywhere along the
portico: a long hall under a high ceiling,
rows of red seats full of people in three blocks with two aisles between, a stage with red curtains and a black grand piano, a pianist at
it; and dark, but for one lamp on the right wall. The door to the wings is in that wall by the
stage, and behind it the panel: a grid of wiring pieces, the feed in the middle. A touch turns a
piece; the power runs from the feed wherever wires meet, and glows where it gets to. When it
gets everywhere (and to the three lamps among the ends) the main switch goes green; thrown, it
puts the lights on. Each panel is grown new, a tree of wires turned about at random, and does not come twice. Her chair, a high one her size, stands in the back row at its left end by the
wall, among the people. With the light the concert begins, whether she sits or not (the people
do); down in her chair the eye is hers, over the rows to the stage: pieces written there and then by a generator
from a bank of short phrases of old keyboard music in the public domain, played through the
phone's own synthesizer, for as long as she stays.

The eighth, papers to the archive: the town hall, its hall and its secretariat, a red folder, the
lift to the roof, the town's helicopter with a brum of black brick at its controls, an arc over
the town and down into the archive's garden, where a skid gives way. The ninth follows at once,
at the archive's door: a duty pickup by the archive to the brums' workshop for a new skid, back,
the skid carried to the helicopter and put on; then she flies it herself, the pilot beside her,
to the pad on the town hall's roof. Two sticks under the thumbs: the left one up and down for
height (let go, it holds it), aside to turn; the right one up and down to go ahead and back,
aside to go sideways. It stands on whatever is level under it, a street or a roof, and is
stopped by what is higher than it; it does not come down on the sea. After that it is hers:
come to its door and stand a moment, and she is at the controls; set down, the button low on the
right lets her out, and it stays where she left it, kept with the game.

The tenth, the containers: a game. In the port on the shore road, past the store, a yard of
containers behind a fence and a gantry crane over it; up the ladder of the tower at the fence's
corner, and she is in the cabin, the eye over the field from far off on a long lens. Touch a
stack and the crane takes its top container up; touch another and it lets it down there, on an
empty bay or on its own colour, no more than four high. Done when every stack is four of one
colour (paid the first time). At the foot of the frame: down out of the cabin, a move taken back,
and once the field is done, a new one brought in, one colour more. Every field is dealt by taking
a sorted one apart with moves that could each be made back, so it always has a way to order.
After the first, the containers are on home's list: straight into the cabin with no way to go,
and back on the sofa when she comes down; or at the crane whenever she climbs it.

The jobs come in turn: home's screen offers the next one, and a list of her own (the games played, the panel) to play again.

What is to come is in `PLAN.md`.

## How it is made

- Java 8 and the platform alone; no build system, no libraries.
- The game lives in plain Java: rules, the town, what stands where. Each frame it fills a
  draw list: the eye, the sun, the sky and every box and ball in the scene. The runner's
  figure is blocks riding on the bones of a humanoid skeleton whose motions are in
  `assets/moves.bin` (see `CREDITS`). The townspeople are textured meshes on the same
  skeleton, when the game has them: they are an asset of their own (`assets/folk.bin` and
  `assets/folk.png`), not part of these sources and not under their license; without them the
  game draws simple figures of its own instead.
- The cars are the game's own: their shapes are boxes in the code, and each wears a skin
  painted for it on its five flat views (from the left, the right, the front, the back and
  above), all in `assets/cars.png`, where each lies in `assets/cars.txt`. The skin is looked up
  by the way a face looks; where it is paint, the car's own paint is laid on in its place.
  The wheels are round, drawn as balls are. The skins are under the same license as the rest.
- The phone draws that list with OpenGL ES 3: the sun's depth map; the sky and the boxes at a
  few pixels to a frame pixel, each material its own worked surface made in the shader, lit by a
  low sun and the sky with soft shadows; then a finishing pass (a soft bloom, the hour's grade,
  the glow of fire) and onto the screen. Meshes go beside the boxes, moved by their bones on the
  graphics chip. The shaders are in `assets/shaders`.
- The desktop stand (`stand/`) plays scenes against the same Java and writes each frame's
  draw list; `stand/web/index.html` draws it with the same shaders in a browser.
